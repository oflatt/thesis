E-Graphs power state-of-the art...
These applications benefit from the e-graph's
  efficient exploration of a large space of equivalences,
  solving (circumventing?) the phase-ordering problem.
Despite these successes, e-graphs
  face numerous challenges when used in more general
  settings.
Programs optimized by the e-graph may
  contain effectful computations,
  contain binders,
  and control flow constructs.
In high-trust settings, proof certificates
  may also be required.
This work presents a practical set of 
  semantic extensions to the e-graph 
  that tackle these issues.
This leads to the thesis claim:
  extended e-graphs are practical in general settings,
  including verified compilers for general programs with effects.
Along the way we'll encounter several
  NP-hard problems and show how 
  designing parameterized algorithms
  leads to performant solutions.
