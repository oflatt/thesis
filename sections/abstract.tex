E-graphs power state-of-the-art program optimizers, synthesizers, and verifiers
  across a variety of domains~\cite{egg,szalinski,caviar}.
Through equality saturation, they explore large spaces of equivalent programs,
  eliminating dependence on rewrite order~\cite{peggy}.
Despite these successes, e-graphs face numerous challenges
  when used in more general settings.
Programs optimized by e-graphs may contain effectful computations,
  binders, and control-flow constructs~\cite{eggcc,slotted-egraphs}.
Applications may also require proofs,
  either to establish correctness~\cite{eggproof}
  or to explain their results~\cite{herbie-proofs}.

This thesis presents semantic extensions to e-graphs that address these challenges.
Its central claim is that \textbf{extended e-graphs are practical and beneficial 
  for program reasoning, including verified compilation
  of effectful programs}.
Along the way, we'll encounter several NP-hard problems and show how
  designing parameterized algorithms leads to performant solutions.
